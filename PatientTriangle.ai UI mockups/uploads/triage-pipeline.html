From door to decision the triage pipeline, one stage at a time

An emergency department turns a patient's first two minutes into a priority. This pipeline does the same — turning sparse arrival data into a recommendation the nurse decides on, and specializing in the one failure that actually harms people: the patient who looks fine but isn't.

The target isn't speed — it's undertriage. Overcrowded EDs mis-sort patients under pressure, and the deadly error is sending a quietly-deteriorating patient to the low-priority queue. Everything here is built around catching that.

Non-negotiables

Three principles the whole design answers to

These are also the exact questions the problem statement tells you to think about — so every slide and every design choice should trace back to one of them.

Decide vs. recommend

The nurse always makes the call

The system recommends a priority and raises flags. It never auto-assigns final acuity. One tap to accept, one to override — the human stays the decision-maker and the accountable party.

Design for the worst case

Tuned against the fatal miss

The safety layer is deliberately asymmetric: it tolerates false alarms to avoid ever missing a deteriorating patient. A false alarm costs minutes; a missed sick patient costs a life.

Humans in the lead

Oversight that stays real

The Over-Trust Guard watches whether nurses genuinely engage or rubber-stamp under load, and calibrates accordingly — so "human-in-the-loop" never quietly becomes reflexive clicking.
